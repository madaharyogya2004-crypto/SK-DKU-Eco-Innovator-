# SK-DKU-Eco-Innovator
An interdisciplinary environmental innovation project focused on identifying ecological challenges and developing practical, sustainable solutions through research and collaborative innovation.

 Overview

The project was a student-led environmental innovation project developed to identify practical environmental and community challenges and explore sustainable, technology-oriented solutions.
The project was conducted as part of the SK DKU Eco Innovator activity, with an emphasis on connecting academic knowledge, creative problem-solving, and social responsibility. Rather than approaching sustainability only as an environmental concept, the project focused on how innovative solutions could address specific problems in everyday life while remaining practical, scalable, and socially beneficial.

Why this project matters?

Environmental challenges are often discussed at a global scale, but many of them are closely connected to problems that communities experience locally.
The project was initiated from the idea that students can contribute to sustainability by identifying such problems and developing solutions based on scientific knowledge, observation, and interdisciplinary thinking.
The activity therefore aimed to:

* Identify an environmental or community problem that requires intervention.

* Understand the underlying causes and consequences of the problem.

* Research existing approaches and their limitations.

* Develop an innovative and practical solution.

* Consider the environmental, social, and technological feasibility of the proposed solution.

* Communicate the proposed solution through collaborative project development and presentation.

Problem Identification

The project began with identifying problems where environmental sustainability, technology, and community well-being intersect.

An important are explored during the activity was sustainable food production.

The area was selected majorly because environmental sustainability extends beyond waste reduction and energy consumption. It also involves responsible interaction with living systems, efficient resource utilization, and developing solutions that can improve community resilience.

Methodology

The project followed an interdisciplinary problem-solving process:

 Step 1 — Problem Identification

Step 2 — Background Research

Step 3 — Solution Development

Step 4 — Innovation and Integration

 Step 5 — Presentation and Evaluation

 Key Outcomes

The project demonstrated how environmental problems can be approached through interdisciplinary innovation rather than through a single scientific or technological discipline.

The work particularly highlighted:

* The importance of identifying the root cause of environmental problems before proposing solutions.

* The role of biotechnology and technology in developing sustainable systems.

* The potential of controlled-environment agriculture, including vertical farming, to address certain limitations of conventional food production.

* The importance of considering social and community needs alongside environmental benefits.

* The value of collaborative problem-solving when developing sustainability-oriented solutions.

Conclusion

The SK DKU Eco Innovator activity provided an opportunity to transform environmental concerns into structured innovation projects.

The project concluded that effective sustainability solutions require more than technological novelty. They must also consider feasibility, resource efficiency, environmental impact, social needs, and long-term implementation.

Through the project, environmental sustainability was approached as an interdisciplinary problem requiring contributions from science, technology, design, and community engagement.

The experience strengthened practical skills in research, problem identification, scientific communication, teamwork, and solution development, while reinforcing an interest in applying biotechnology and interdisciplinary innovation to real-world challenges.

Potential areas for further investigation include:

* Measuring environmental impact and resource efficiency.

* Developing prototypes or experimental models.

* Conducting feasibility and cost analyses.

* Evaluating scalability in different environments.

* Integrating sensors, automation, or data-driven monitoring.

* Assessing long-term environmental and social outcomes.
